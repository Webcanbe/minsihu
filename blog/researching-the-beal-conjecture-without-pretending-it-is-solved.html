Researching the Beal Conjecture Without Pretending It Is Solved

Mathematical research log · September 2026

I have been running a long-form research project on the Beal Conjecture. The goal is intentionally strict: a complete general proof, or an explicit primitive counterexample. Anything else can be useful mathematics, but it is not a solution.

That sounds obvious. In practice, keeping that standard is one of the hardest parts of working on a famous open problem.

Label every claim

I began using explicit states for results: KNOWN for established literature, DERIVED for conclusions I can justify from accepted inputs, CANDIDATE for promising but incomplete ideas, and REFUTED when an argument fails.

The labels prevent a common research failure: an attractive intermediate observation slowly becoming “basically proved” through repetition.

Primary sources over memory

A large part of the work has been literature verification rather than symbolic manipulation. When a lemma matters, I try to locate the original statement, inspect the hypotheses, and check whether the normalization, coprimality assumptions, exceptional primes, and exponent conditions match the case I am studying.

That process has killed several directions, which is useful. A failed route that is clearly documented is better than an ambiguous route that keeps consuming attention.

Progress can mean narrowing the unknown

Open problems create a temptation to measure progress by how close an argument feels to the final theorem. I prefer a different measure: how much uncertainty has been removed.

If a branch of cases is rigorously eliminated, if a claimed theorem turns out not to apply, or if a proposed reduction depends on an unproved global bound, the project becomes more accurate even if it does not become more dramatic.

I have not solved the Beal Conjecture. The project is valuable to me because it forces a level of epistemic discipline that transfers directly into engineering and business: do not confuse a promising mechanism with a finished result.
