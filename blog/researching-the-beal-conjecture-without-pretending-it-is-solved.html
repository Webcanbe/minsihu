Researching the Beal Conjecture Without Pretending It Is Solved

Research started 25 Mar 2026 · ongoing

On 25 March 2026 I started a dedicated long-form research project on the Beal Conjecture. The success condition is intentionally strict: a complete general proof, or an explicit primitive counterexample. Anything else can be useful mathematics, but it is not a solution.

Label every claim

I use explicit states for results: KNOWN for established literature, DERIVED for conclusions justified from accepted inputs, CANDIDATE for promising but incomplete ideas, and REFUTED when an argument fails.

The labels prevent an attractive intermediate observation from slowly turning into “basically proved” through repetition.

Primary sources over memory

A large part of the work has been theorem and hypothesis auditing. When a lemma matters, I try to locate the original source and check whether coprimality, exponent conditions, normalization, exceptional primes, and the exact object being raised to a power really match the case under study.

The research has moved through exponent and valuation reductions, Frey-curve and modular approaches, multi-Frey literature, effective finiteness, S-unit and height bounds, primitive-divisor mechanisms, and repeated attempts to identify a genuinely uniform obstruction.

Failed routes stay in the record

A direction that depends on an unproved global bound is not quietly upgraded into a theorem. A literature claim that does not apply to the required family is marked and removed from the main attack.

I have not solved the Beal Conjecture. The value of the project is that it forces every step to survive hostile checking before it is allowed to support the next one.
