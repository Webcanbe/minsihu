From Inventory Software to Operations

Project started 30 Jan 2026

On 30 January 2026 I started an inventory-software project. The first version looked like the category usually looks: products, stock levels, suppliers, alerts, and synchronization.

The useful shift came when I asked what the user was actually paying to avoid. The answer was not “looking at inventory.” It was the operational work created by inventory: noticing risk, deciding what to order, approving a purchase, and keeping store data synchronized.

From dashboard to workflow

I worked through store integration and Shopify API/OAuth concerns, moved away from mock data, and modeled concrete inventory states. Alerts became tied to conditions such as safety stock, lead time, and predicted stockout.

The more important step was turning information into an action: generate a reorder proposal, let a human approve it, and keep the workflow traceable.

Manual can be a feature in an MVP

I stopped treating every manual step as technical debt. Early in a product, a manual sync or approval can be the fastest way to validate the workflow before spending time automating something users may not need.

The lesson

A dashboard tells you what happened. Operations software helps decide what happens next. That distinction carried into later products.
