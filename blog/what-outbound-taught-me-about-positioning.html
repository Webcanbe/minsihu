What Outbound Taught Me About Positioning

Operating notes · September 2026

Outbound is easy to reduce to volume: find leads, send messages, measure replies. The useful part for me was different. It forced every vague sentence about WebCanBe to become concrete.

If I could not explain who the product was for, what problem it solved, and why that person should care in a short message, the positioning was probably not ready.

Specificity beats broad ambition

Early versions of the pitch moved across creators, agencies, founders, software teams, and investors. Every audience made sense in isolation, which was exactly the problem. A message that can be sent to everyone usually describes a category instead of a sharp pain.

Agency outreach worked best when the role was explicit: a production arm that could take overflow work without trying to take over the client relationship. Product-focused outreach became clearer when the message centered on visual editing that changes the real code.

Usage signals matter more than presentation

One lesson that kept appearing was that real reuse is more valuable than superficial approval. A rating can look good. A polished deck can look good. But repeated imports, edits, deployments, forks, exports, and returning users tell you whether the product is becoming part of someone’s workflow.

That changed what I wanted to measure. Instead of treating attention as the finish line, I started thinking more about behavior: Did someone use the project again? Did they keep the code? Did they ship something? Did they come back?

Outbound as product research

The best version of outbound is not a separate sales machine sitting next to the product. It is a fast feedback system for understanding where the product becomes obviously useful and where the explanation still feels forced.

I still care about distribution. But I now see a good outbound system as a way to test the edges of positioning before they become expensive assumptions.
