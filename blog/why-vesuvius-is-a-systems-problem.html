Why Vesuvius Is a Systems Problem

Vesuvius Challenge project · September 2026

The Vesuvius Challenge looks, from a distance, like an image recognition problem: find ink inside scans of carbonized scrolls. The closer I looked, the more it became a systems problem.

Reading a scroll that cannot be physically opened requires a chain of steps. You need volumetric data, geometric reconstruction, surface extraction, flattening or unwrapping, ink detection, and a way to present the result so a human can verify that the letters are actually preserved rather than invented by interpolation.

The pipeline is the result

A strong model is not enough if the surface geometry is wrong. A clean rendering is not enough if the unwrapping process destroys local structure. A readable patch is not enough if the process cannot be reproduced from the disclosed data, checkpoints, and code.

The challenge rules make this explicit. Human input is constrained. Training and prediction regions cannot overlap improperly. Some higher-resolution shortcuts are disallowed. The final output has to preserve a large share of the characters in a way that can be inspected.

Interfaces matter in research

One of the most interesting lessons for me is how much research quality depends on tools for inspecting intermediate states. If a researcher cannot quickly compare a 3D surface, a flattened patch, and an ink prediction, mistakes can travel through the pipeline unnoticed.

That makes visualization, coordinate systems, metadata, and reproducible export formats part of the scientific method rather than secondary engineering.

Why I keep it as a long-term project

The project combines several things I like: hard evidence, software systems, computer vision, geometry, and a clear external standard for success. It is also a useful antidote to vague progress. Either the recovered characters are preserved and legible under the rules, or they are not.

I am still in the research and systems-design phase. The point is not to claim a discovery early. The point is to build a pipeline capable of producing one that can survive scrutiny.
