Why Vesuvius Is a Systems Problem

Project started 2 Feb 2026 · ongoing

I started this project on 2 February 2026. The Vesuvius Challenge looks from a distance like an ink-detection problem. The closer I looked, the more it became a full systems problem.

Reading a scroll that cannot be physically opened requires a chain: volumetric data, geometric reconstruction, surface extraction, flattening or unwrapping, ink prediction, and a result that a human can inspect without trusting hidden interpolation.

The pipeline is the result

A strong model is not enough if the surface geometry is wrong. A clean rendering is not enough if unwrapping damages local structure. A readable patch is not enough if the process cannot be reproduced from the disclosed data, checkpoints, logs, and code.

The current challenge rules make that explicit through limits on human input, train/prediction separation, resolution constraints, character-preservation requirements, and reproducibility expectations.

Interfaces matter in research

A researcher needs to compare intermediate states: the 3D surface, extracted patch, flattened representation, coordinate mapping, and ink prediction. If those states are difficult to inspect, errors can travel through the pipeline unnoticed.

That makes visualization and metadata part of the scientific method rather than secondary engineering.

Why I keep it long term

The project combines hard evidence, geometry, computer vision, software systems, and a clear external standard for success. I am interested in building a pipeline that can survive scrutiny, not merely produce an attractive screenshot.
