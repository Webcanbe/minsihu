Recovery Is Part of Ownership

Source recovery notes · September 2026

Source ownership sounds simple until the working copy becomes difficult to access.

During one WebCanBe development phase, a repository living inside a cloud-synced documents location hit a macOS dataless-file problem. Parts of the project were present logically but not reliably available as normal local files.

Ownership needs a recovery path

I recovered the active work into a local non-cloud-synced clone, preserved the relevant source and configuration files, and verified the recovered files rather than assuming the copy was complete.

The incident changed a product principle for me. Export is not enough. If a project is genuinely owned, its state should be reconstructable from understandable source, version history, and portable assets without depending on one editor or sync layer.

Reliability is architecture, not cleanup

Recovery procedures are usually treated as operational housekeeping. I think they belong in product architecture. Versioning, explicit project contracts, portable source, and deterministic rebuild steps reduce the number of situations where “the project exists” but cannot be confidently reconstructed.

That matters for WebCanBe because source ownership is one of the product's core claims. A claim like that should survive failure, not only work on the happy path.
